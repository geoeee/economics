Inflation & Deflation / 通胀与通缩

The silent thief of purchasing power — and why central banks fear falling prices more than rising ones.

📈 Economics / 经济学

What Is Inflation? / 什么是通胀？

Inflation is a sustained increase in the general price level of goods and services in an economy over time. It is measured primarily through:

  • CPI (Consumer Price Index): basket of consumer goods and services
  • PPI (Producer Price Index): wholesale prices at the production level
  • GDP Deflator: broadest measure, captures all domestically produced goods
  • Core inflation: CPI excluding volatile food and energy prices

Causes / 成因

Demand-pull / 需求拉动 Too much money chasing too few goods. Classic M2 growth above GDP growth.
Cost-push / 成本推动 Rising input costs (oil, wages, supply chain) push prices up.
Built-in / 惯性通胀 Inflation expectations become self-fulfilling; workers demand wage hikes → firms raise prices → loop.
Monetary / 货币性 Excessive money supply growth (Milton Friedman: "inflation is always and everywhere a monetary phenomenon").

Deflation: The Real Monster / 通缩：真正的恶魔

Deflation (falling prices) sounds appealing but is economically devastating. It creates a deflationary spiral: consumers delay purchases (expecting lower prices tomorrow) → demand collapses → firms cut production and wages → less income → even less demand → repeat. This was the dynamic of the Great Depression and Japan's Lost Decades.

Central banks target ~2% inflation — not 0% — to maintain a buffer against deflation. At 0%, a shock could tip the economy into a deflationary spiral that monetary policy (already at zero rates) cannot easily stop.
⚡ Politics / 政治

Inflation as a Political Weapon / 通胀作为政治武器

Inflation redistributes wealth — from creditors to debtors, from savers to borrowers. Governments with large debts benefit from moderate inflation (it erodes the real value of debt). This creates an inherent conflict: central banks want price stability; politicians want growth and manageable debt ratios.

Inflation Targeting / 通胀目标制

Since New Zealand pioneered it in 1990, most major central banks now operate under inflation targeting — publicly committing to ~2% inflation. This anchors expectations and provides accountability. Critics argue it's arbitrary (why 2%?) and can blind central banks to asset bubbles (the 2008 crisis built up under "low and stable" CPI).

💰 Finance / 金融

Inflation is the arch-enemy of fixed-income investors. A bond yielding 4% during 3% inflation has a real yield of only ~1%. This is why inflation-linked bonds (TIPS in the US, linkers in the UK) adjust principal for CPI.

Real assets (real estate, commodities, gold) are traditional inflation hedges. Equities provide partial protection (companies can raise prices), but high inflation compresses P/E multiples because future earnings are discounted at higher rates.

The breakeven rate — the difference between nominal and inflation-linked bond yields — is the market's expectation of future inflation. It's one of the most-watched indicators in global finance.

📐 Mathematics / 数学

Inflation Rate / 通胀率

$$\pi = \frac{\text{CPI}_t - \text{CPI}_{t-1}}{\text{CPI}_{t-1}} \times 100\%$$

Fisher Equation / 费雪方程式

$$\begin{aligned} r &= i - \pi \\[4pt] r &= \text{real interest rate} \\ i &= \text{nominal interest rate} \\ \pi &= \text{inflation rate} \\[4pt] &\text{Example: loan at } 5\% \text{ with } 3\% \text{ inflation } \rightarrow \text{ real cost } = 2\% \end{aligned}$$

Rule of 70 / 70法则

$$\begin{aligned} \text{Years to double price level} &= \frac{70}{\text{inflation rate(\%)}} \\[4pt] \text{At } 2\% \text{ inflation: prices double every } &\sim 35 \text{ years} \\ \text{At } 7\% \text{ inflation: prices double every } &\sim 10 \text{ years} \end{aligned}$$

Quantity Equation / 货币数量方程

$$\begin{aligned} MV = PY \;&\rightarrow\; \%\Delta M + \%\Delta V \approx \%\Delta P + \%\Delta Y \\[4pt] &\text{If } V \text{ stable and } Y \text{ grows at trend: excess } M \text{ growth } \rightarrow \text{ inflation} \end{aligned}$$
🧠 Psychology / 心理
  • Inflation expectations / 通胀预期: the most important driver of actual inflation. If people expect 5% inflation, they demand 5% wage increases and firms preemptively raise prices — expectations become self-fulfilling. Anchoring expectations is the central bank's primary psychological task.
  • Salience bias / 显著性偏差: people notice price increases in frequently purchased items (groceries, gas) more than occasional purchases (appliances, cars), leading to perceived inflation diverging from measured inflation.
  • Loss aversion / 损失厌恶: consumers experience rising prices as losses from a reference point, producing disproportionate anger. This explains why moderate inflation feels worse than equivalent nominal wage gains feel good.
  • Zombie firms / 僵尸企业: low inflation environments allow unproductive firms to survive on cheap debt; moderate inflation accelerates "creative destruction" by exposing weak business models.
