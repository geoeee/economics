Market Structures / 市场结构

From perfect competition to monopoly — the spectrum of market power and its implications for prices, efficiency, and welfare.

Perfect Competition / 完全竞争

A theoretical benchmark where P = MC (price equals marginal cost). Allocatively and productively efficient, zero economic profit in long run. Almost never exists in pure form — farming and commodity markets come closest. Its value is as a reference point to measure inefficiency.

Monopoly / 垄断

Single seller sets price above marginal cost (P > MC), creating deadweight loss. Natural monopolies (utilities, railways) arise when fixed costs are so high that one firm can serve the entire market at lower average cost than multiple firms.

Oligopoly / 寡头

The most prevalent structure in modern capitalism: airlines, telecoms, tech platforms, banks. Strategic interdependence — each firm's decisions depend on competitors' anticipated responses — makes analysis complex. Enter game theory. Key outcomes: collusion (like a monopoly, illegal in most jurisdictions), price wars, or tacit coordination.

⚡ Politics / 政治

Antitrust / 反垄断

The political-legal response to market power. Different jurisdictions take different approaches:

  • US: Sherman Act (1890), Clayton Act (1914) — focuses on consumer welfare (prices). Recent neo-Brandeisian movement (Lina Khan) shifts toward market structure concerns.
  • EU: More aggressive on regulating dominant firms; DMA (Digital Markets Act, 2022) preemptively regulates Big Tech gatekeepers.
  • China: Anti-Monopoly Law (2008); enforcement intensified dramatically in 2021 against tech platforms (Alibaba fined $2.8B).
The fundamental tension: some monopolies arise from superior efficiency or innovation (Schumpeter); others from anti-competitive practices. Distinguishing between them is the central challenge of antitrust.
📜 History / 历史
1600–1858
British East India Company
State-chartered monopoly on trade with Asia. Controlled territory, armies, taxation — corporation as empire.
1870–1911
Standard Oil
Rockefeller's monopoly controlled ~90% of US oil refining. Broken up by Supreme Court in 1911.
1984
AT&T breakup
Telecom monopoly split into 7 "Baby Bells." Launched competitive telecom market.
1998–2001
US v. Microsoft
Landmark tech antitrust case over browser bundling. Microsoft avoided breakup on appeal.
2020–present
Big Tech antitrust revival
Google, Apple, Meta, Amazon face investigations in US, EU, and China.
💰 Finance / 金融

Market structure directly affects corporate valuation. A firm with moat (sustainable competitive advantage — Buffett's term) commands premium P/E ratios. Concentration ratios (CR4, CR8) and Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI) are used by regulators and investors alike to assess market power risk.

Network effects create winner-take-most dynamics in platform markets: a marketplace is more valuable when it has more users, creating self-reinforcing monopolies (Meta, Uber, Airbnb). These aren't traditional monopolies but natural monopolies of data and network.

📐 Mathematics / 数学

Profit Maximization / 利润最大化

$$\begin{aligned} &MR = MC \quad \text{(Marginal Revenue = Marginal Cost)} \\[4pt] &\text{For monopoly:} \\ &MR = P\left(1 - \frac{1}{|E_d|}\right) \quad \text{where } E_d = \text{price elasticity of demand} \\ &P = \frac{MC}{1 - \frac{1}{|E_d|}} \quad \leftarrow \text{markup above cost} \end{aligned}$$

Herfindahl-Hirschman Index / 赫芬达尔指数

$$\begin{aligned} \text{HHI} &= \sum (s_i)^2 \times 10{,}000 \\[4pt] \text{where } s_i &= \text{market share of firm } i \\[4pt] \text{HHI} < 1500 \;&\rightarrow\; \text{Unconcentrated} \\ 1500 \leq \text{HHI} < 2500 \;&\rightarrow\; \text{Moderately concentrated} \\ \text{HHI} \geq 2500 \;&\rightarrow\; \text{Highly concentrated (scrutiny on mergers)} \end{aligned}$$

Lerner Index / 勒纳指数

$$\begin{aligned} L &= \frac{P - MC}{P} \\[4pt] L = 0 \;&\rightarrow\; \text{Perfect competition} \\ L \approx 1 \;&\rightarrow\; \text{Monopoly} \\ &\text{Measures pricing power directly} \end{aligned}$$
🧠 Psychology / 心理
  • Brand loyalty as a barrier / 品牌忠诚即壁垒: consumers pay a premium for branded goods even when chemically identical generics exist — creating monopolistic competition purely through psychology.
  • Default effects / 默认效应: in platform markets, users rarely switch from defaults (Google Search, iOS default apps), entrenching dominant positions without explicit anti-competitive behavior.
  • Perceived competition / 感知竞争: markets feel more competitive when there are more visible choices, even if concentration is high. This is why monopolistic competition (many differentiated products) feels better to consumers than oligopoly, even when outcomes are similar.
