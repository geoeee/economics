Chinese Economic History / 中国经济史

From the world's largest economy for most of recorded history, through collapse and reinvention — how China's economic past shapes its present.

📈 Economics / 经济学

The Great Divergence / 大分流

For most of recorded history, China was the world's largest economy. As late as 1820, China accounted for ~33% of global GDP (Angus Maddison estimates). Then came the Great Divergence: between 1800–1950, Western Europe and North America surged ahead while China stagnated and collapsed. Why? The debate encompasses institutions (Needham's paradox: why did the Scientific Revolution happen in Europe, not China?), geography, colonialism, and state structure.

The Reform Era / 改革开放

After Mao's death (1976), Deng Xiaoping launched economic reforms in 1978. Key elements of the Socialist Market Economy:

  • Agricultural decollectivization: Household Responsibility System replaced communes; grain output surged
  • Township and Village Enterprises (TVEs): local government-run businesses bridged plan and market
  • Special Economic Zones: Shenzhen transformed from fishing village to global tech hub (pop. 17M)
  • Dual-track system: planned prices for quotas, market prices for above-quota production — gradual transition without collapse
  • WTO accession (2001): integrated China into global supply chains; 10%+ GDP growth sustained for decades
Unprecedented achievement: From 1978 to 2020, China lifted ~800 million people out of extreme poverty — the largest and fastest poverty reduction in human history.
⚡ Politics / 政治

The Chinese State in the Economy

Key institutions shaping China's political economy:

SOEs / 国有企业 State-Owned Enterprises still control ~40% of economic assets. Dominant in energy, telecoms, finance, infrastructure. "Grasp the large, let go of the small."
NDRC / 发改委 National Development and Reform Commission — the economic planning nerve center. Sets industrial policy, approves major projects, controls prices in key sectors.
Local Government Financing Provinces/cities borrow heavily through LGFVs (Local Government Financing Vehicles). Land sales (~40% of local revenue) created a property-centric fiscal model.
Industrial Policy "Made in China 2025" targeted dominance in AI, semiconductors, EVs, biotech. Provoked Western backlash and export controls.

Central-Local Dynamics

A defining tension: Beijing sets broad policy but local officials control implementation. Tournament-style promotion incentives (GDP growth → promotion) drove the investment boom but also created perverse incentives (ghost cities, hidden debt, environmental degradation). The property crisis (2021–) exposed the fragility of the land-finance model.

📜 History / 历史
221 BC – 1911
Imperial Economy / 帝国经济
State monopolies on salt and iron (Han Dynasty). Grand Canal connecting north-south. Paper money invented (Song, 11th C) — and later hyperinflated into worthlessness.
1840–1949
Century of Humiliation / 百年屈辱
Opium Wars, unequal treaties, foreign concessions. Self-strengthening movement failed. Economic collapse, hyperinflation (1948: prices doubled every ~15 hours).
1949–1978
Planned Economy / 计划经济
Soviet-style central planning. Great Leap Forward (1958–62) caused catastrophic famine (15–45M excess deaths). Cultural Revolution disrupted education and economy.
1978–1993
Reform Without a Blueprint
"Crossing the river by feeling the stones." Gradual, experimental reforms. Dual-track pricing. SEZs proved market mechanisms could work in a socialist framework.
1993–2012
Marketization & Globalization
Deng's 1992 Southern Tour revitalized reforms. SOE restructuring (1997) laid off ~40M workers. WTO (2001) triggered export boom. GDP grew ~10%/year.
2012–present
New Era / 新时代
Xi Jinping: state's role expanded. Belt and Road launched (2013). Tech crackdown (2021). Property crisis. "Common Prosperity." De-risking of supply chains.
💰 Finance / 金融

China's financial system is bank-dominated (banks provide ~80% of credit, vs ~30% in the US). The "Big Four" state banks (ICBC, CCB, ABC, BOC) are the world's largest by assets. Stock markets (Shanghai, Shenzhen) are relatively young (1990) and historically treated as policy tools, not efficient allocators of capital.

The shadow banking system exploded in the 2010s — wealth management products, trust loans, entrusted loans — creating ~$8T of off-balance-sheet credit. Regulatory crackdown (2017–) brought it under control but contributed to the property sector liquidity crisis (Evergrande, Country Garden).

Capital controls remain the firewall: the RMB is not freely convertible on the capital account. This insulates China from the currency crises that hit other emerging markets but limits the yuan's global role.

📐 Mathematics / 数学

Cobb-Douglas with Chinese Characteristics

Y = A × K^α × L^β × H^γ

K = physical capital (massive infrastructure investment)
L = labor (demographic dividend, now shrinking)
H = human capital (college enrollment surged from 4% to 60%)
A = TFP (Total Factor Productivity — the key question:
     can China transition from investment-driven to
     innovation-driven growth?)

Lewis Turning Point / 刘易斯拐点

When surplus rural labor is exhausted:
  wages rise → consumption share of GDP increases →
  investment-led growth model must shift

China likely passed this point around 2010–2015
Working-age population peaked in 2013, began declining
🧠 Psychology / 心理
  • High savings rate / 高储蓄率: Chinese households save ~35% of income — among the world's highest. Driven by precautionary motives (weak social safety net), housing down payments, and education costs. Cultural emphasis on thrift and intergenerational support.
  • Property obsession / 房产执念: homeownership rate ~90%, among the world's highest. Housing seen as the essential asset for marriage, status, and retirement. This psychological anchoring to real estate is both a cause and consequence of the property-driven economic model.
  • Century of Humiliation as psychological scar: collective memory of subjugation drives the pursuit of economic and technological self-sufficiency. "Never again dependent on foreigners" is economic policy rooted in historical trauma.
