Financial Crises / 金融危机

Why financial systems periodically implode, taking the real economy with them — and why we never seem to learn the lesson.

📈 Economics / 经济学

Minsky's Financial Instability Hypothesis / 明斯基假说

Hyman Minsky argued that stability is destabilizing. Periods of prosperity encourage risk-taking; debt accumulates; asset prices rise; confidence grows — until the system becomes fragile and a shock triggers collapse. The three stages of borrowers:

Hedge / 对冲型 Can repay both principal and interest from cash flow — safest
Speculative / 投机型 Can pay interest but must roll over principal — vulnerable to rate rises
Ponzi / 庞氏型 Cannot even pay interest — must borrow more or sell assets — collapse imminent
Minsky Moment: the point when over-indebted investors are forced to sell assets to meet margin calls, causing a cascading collapse in asset prices — a self-reinforcing doom loop.

Common Crisis Patterns / 危机的共同模式

  1. Displacement: a new technology, policy, or opportunity creates excitement (railways, internet, subprime mortgages, crypto)
  2. Boom: credit expands, asset prices rise, euphoria builds
  3. Mania: speculation detaches from fundamentals; "this time is different" narratives dominate
  4. Distress: insiders sell; cracks appear; true state becomes undeniable
  5. Revulsion / Panic: crash, bankruptcies, contagion — the system seizes up

This pattern, outlined by Charles Kindleberger, repeats with uncanny regularity across centuries. The details change; the psychology doesn't.

⚡ Politics / 政治

Bailouts and Moral Hazard / 救助与道德风险

When crises hit, governments face the "too big to fail" dilemma: let banks collapse (contagion destroys the economy) or bail them out (rewards recklessness, ensures future crises). There is no clean choice — only varying degrees of bad.

Crisis as Political Opportunity / 危机即政治机会

Rahm Emanuel: "Never let a good crisis go to waste." The 2008 crisis enabled Dodd-Frank, Basel III, and the creation of the CFPB. The 2020 COVID crisis enabled unprecedented fiscal expansion. Crises concentrate political will that is normally too diffuse to overcome vested interests.

Who Pays? / 谁买单？

Crises disproportionately harm the poor and middle class — unemployment spikes, homes are foreclosed — while bailouts disproportionately benefit financial elites who own the assets being rescued. This asymmetry fueled the Occupy Wall Street movement and the broader populist backlash of the 2010s.

💰 Finance / 金融

Mechanisms of Contagion / 传染机制

  • Bank runs: depositors panic and withdraw simultaneously — banks can't liquidate long-term assets fast enough
  • Fire sales: forced selling drives asset prices below fundamental value, triggering margin calls on others holding the same assets
  • Counterparty risk: failure of one institution threatens all who have unsettled contracts with it (Lehman Brothers, 2008)
  • Sovereign-bank doom loop: weak banks weaken sovereign credit → sovereign bonds held by banks lose value → banks weaken further (Eurozone crisis)

Warning Signs / 预警信号

Credit growth Rapid private credit expansion consistently predicts crises
Asset price bubbles Prices significantly above long-term trend (Shiller CAPE, price-to-rent)
Yield curve inversion Short-term rates above long-term → recession signal (8/8 since 1950)
Current account deficits Large, persistent deficits → vulnerable to sudden capital flow reversal
📐 Mathematics / 数学

Present Value and Asset Bubbles / 现值与资产泡沫

Rational Price = Σ(CF_t / (1+r)^t)  for t = 1→n

CF_t = expected cash flow at time t
r = discount rate

A bubble exists when: Market Price ≫ Σ(CF_t / (1+r)^t)

i.e. the price cannot be justified by reasonable expectations
of future cash flows discounted at a reasonable rate

Leverage and Amplification / 杠杆与放大

Simple leverage:  L = Assets / Equity

With L = 10 (10:1 leverage):
  10% asset rise → doubles your equity (gain 100%)
  10% asset fall → wipes out your equity (loss 100%)

Leverage amplifies both gains AND losses symmetrically

Value at Risk (VaR) / 在险价值

VaR(95%) = minimum loss in the worst 5% of scenarios

Problem: VaR assumes normal distributions, ignores tail risk.
In a crisis, correlation → 1 (all assets fall together).
Models that assume diversification become catastrophically wrong.
🧠 Psychology / 心理
  • "This time is different" / 这次不一样: the four most dangerous words in finance (Reinhart & Rogoff). Every bubble has believers who argue the old rules no longer apply — the internet, housing never goes down nationally, crypto is a new paradigm. It's never different.
  • Herding / 羊群效应: investors follow the crowd because (a) if everyone is wrong, you won't be blamed; (b) seeing others profit creates FOMO. This amplifies bubbles on the way up and crashes on the way down.
  • Overconfidence / 过度自信: after a long bull market, people confuse luck with skill. "I'm a genius" in 2021 → "the system is rigged" in 2022.
  • Disaster myopia / 灾难短视: the longer it's been since the last crisis, the more the market underprices risk. By 2007, it had been ~70 years since a systemic banking crisis in the US. Risk models were calibrated on calm data.
  • Narrative economics / 叙事经济学: Robert Shiller's insight that contagious stories — not just fundamentals — drive economic behavior. Bitcoin, AI, housing — all have powerful narratives that sustain booms long after numbers stop making sense.
