Contemporary / 当代

The era of polycrisis: the Global Financial Crisis shattered economic orthodoxies, the pandemic rewrote the rules of government intervention, and the post-Cold War order is being replaced — by what, no one yet knows.

📈 Economics / 经济学

The Global Financial Crisis (2008) / 全球金融危机

The worst economic disaster since the Great Depression. Causes: a housing bubble fueled by low interest rates, financial deregulation, and a flood of global savings; subprime mortgages packaged into complex securities (MBS, CDOs) rated AAA by agencies paid by issuers; leverage ratios of 30:1 at major investment banks; shadow banking that had grown larger than regulated banking. When housing prices turned, the entire structure collapsed. Lehman Brothers' bankruptcy (September 15, 2008) triggered a global panic. The US lost ~$10T in household wealth. Unemployment doubled to 10%. The eurozone crisis followed (2010–2012), nearly destroying the single currency.

QE and the Zero Lower Bound / 量化宽松与零利率下限

Central banks responded with unprecedented monetary expansion. The Fed cut rates to zero (December 2008) and launched three rounds of QE, expanding its balance sheet from ~$900B to ~$4.5T. The ECB, Bank of Japan, and Bank of England followed. QE "worked" — it prevented depression, stabilized markets, and (eventually) restored growth. But it also had profound side effects: asset prices soared (benefiting the wealthy who own assets), income inequality widened, and the financial system became addicted to cheap money. The exit from QE proved more difficult than the entry.

Inequality and the Piketty Moment / 不平等与皮凯蒂时刻

Thomas Piketty's Capital in the Twenty-First Century (2014) became an unlikely global bestseller. Its central argument: when the rate of return on capital (r) exceeds the rate of economic growth (g) — r > g — wealth concentrates, and inherited wealth dominates earned income. This isn't an aberration of capitalism; it's the default tendency. The data showed inequality in the US and Europe returning to pre-WWI levels. Piketty's work transformed the inequality debate from a moral argument into an empirical one — and sparked a backlash from orthodox economists who disputed his methodology and conclusions.

The top 1% in the US now hold more wealth than the entire middle class. CEO-to-worker pay ratio: ~20:1 in 1965 → ~350:1 in 2022. Whether this is a problem for growth (Piketty) or merely a political problem (Summers) is the central debate in contemporary economics.

COVID-19: The Policy Revolution / 新冠政策革命

The pandemic response shattered remaining fiscal and monetary taboos. The US ran a ~$3T deficit (2020 — 15% of GDP). Governments worldwide paid workers to stay home (furlough schemes). Central banks bought everything: government bonds, corporate bonds, even bond ETFs. The response worked: unemployment spiked but recovered rapidly; mass bankruptcies were avoided. But the aftermath was inflation — the first sustained inflation in developed economies in ~40 years — leading to the most aggressive rate-hiking cycle since the 1980s.

⚡ Politics / 政治

Populism and Economic Backlash / 民粹主义与经济反弹

Brexit (2016) and Trump's election (2016) were economic events as much as political ones. Decades of globalization had created concentrated losers (manufacturing workers in rich countries) and diffuse winners (consumers everywhere, capital owners). The losers finally organized politically. The backlash was against free trade, immigration, technocratic institutions (EU, WTO, central banks), and "elites" generally. This wasn't irrational — it was the political expression of economic grievances that mainstream economics had dismissed for decades.

US-China Decoupling / 中美脱钩

The post-Cold War assumption that economic integration would produce political convergence has collapsed. Instead, the US and China are engaged in a multidimensional competition: tariffs (US raised average tariffs on Chinese goods from ~3% to ~19%), technology controls (semiconductor export bans, Huawei blacklisting, TikTok divestment pressure), and supply chain restructuring (reshoring, friend-shoring, "China + 1" strategies). The era of "hyperglobalization" is over; the era of "managed trade" has begun. The question is whether this managed competition can avoid escalating into full economic warfare.

Climate Economics / 气候经济学

Climate change has moved from environmental concern to central economic challenge. The transition to net-zero will require the largest reallocation of capital in history — estimated at $3–5T annually through 2050. Carbon pricing (taxes or cap-and-trade systems) is the economists' consensus solution; political opposition to higher energy prices has blocked it in most countries. The EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM, 2023) — a tariff on imports from countries without carbon pricing — is a new frontier in the intersection of trade, climate, and geopolitics.

💰 Finance / 金融

Cryptocurrency and DeFi / 加密货币与去中心化金融

Bitcoin (2009) was born from the financial crisis — a direct response to the failure of centralized financial institutions. Ethereum (2015) added programmability, spawning DeFi (decentralized finance): lending, trading, and insurance without intermediaries. By 2021, crypto market cap exceeded $3T. By 2022, it had collapsed to ~$800B. FTX, a ~$32B exchange, was revealed as a fraud. The crypto experiment is ongoing: it has demonstrated that decentralized systems can function at scale, but also that they replicate — and sometimes amplify — the speculative excesses of traditional finance.

Bank Failures 2.0 / 银行倒闭2.0

March 2023: Silicon Valley Bank (SVB) collapsed in 48 hours — the largest US bank failure since 2008. The cause was classic: a bank run (depositors withdrew $42B in a single day), but accelerated by Twitter and mobile banking apps. SVB had invested deposits in long-duration bonds; when rates rose, those bonds lost value. The Federal Reserve and FDIC guaranteed all deposits (even uninsured). The speed of the collapse — faster than any previous bank run — demonstrated that financial panic now moves at the speed of social media.

Rise of Private Markets / 私募市场的崛起

Public markets are shrinking. The number of US-listed companies fell from ~8,000 (1996) to ~4,000 (2023). Private equity, venture capital, and private credit have absorbed the difference. This shift concentrates wealth among institutional investors, reduces transparency, and raises questions about how capitalism allocates capital when most companies stay private.

📐 Mathematics / 数学

Piketty's r > g / 皮凯蒂的 r > g

$$\text{If } r \text{ (return on capital)} > g \text{ (growth rate):}$$ $$\rightarrow \text{capital/income ratio } \beta = \frac{s}{g} \text{ rises } (s = \text{savings rate})$$ $$\rightarrow \text{wealth concentrates}$$ $$\rightarrow \text{inherited wealth dominates earned income}$$ $$\text{Piketty's data: } r \approx 4\text{–}5\% \text{ historically}$$ $$\quad g \approx 1\text{–}2\% \text{ in mature economies}$$ $$\rightarrow r > g \text{ is the historical norm, not exception}$$ $$\text{Implication: capitalism tends naturally toward inequality unless interrupted by war, revolution, or policy intervention}$$

Modern Monetary Theory (MMT) / 现代货币理论

$$\text{For a monetarily sovereign nation:}$$ $$\text{Government spending is NOT constrained by tax revenue}$$ $$\text{Real constraint: inflation, not solvency}$$ $$\text{The government can always "print" money to pay debts.}$$ $$\text{The question is not "can we afford it?" but "will it cause inflation?"}$$ $$\text{Job Guarantee: government offers employment at living wage to anyone willing to work — automatic stabilizer + price anchor}$$

R-star (Natural Rate of Interest) / 自然利率

$$r^* = \text{the real interest rate consistent with full employment and stable inflation}$$ $$\text{Estimated to have fallen from} \sim 2\% \text{ (1990s) to} \sim 0.5\% \text{ (2010s)}$$ $$\rightarrow \text{secular stagnation (Summers)}$$ $$\rightarrow \text{"lower for longer" era for interest rates}$$ $$\text{If } r^* \text{ is truly near zero, central banks have very limited room to cut rates in the next recession}$$ $$\rightarrow \text{the post-2022 rate hikes may be temporary}$$
🧠 Psychology / 心理
  • Trust collapse / 信任崩溃: the 2008 crisis destroyed public trust in financial institutions, governments, and economic expertise itself. The recovery was slow and uneven, breeding resentment that fueled populism. Trust, once lost, is extraordinarily difficult to rebuild — and the continuing stream of scandals (Libor, forex rigging, FTX) has prevented rebuilding.
  • Doom-scrolling and the speed of panic / 恐慌的社交媒体速度: the SVB run demonstrated that financial panics now move at internet speed. Venture capitalists tweeted their fears → founders withdrew funds on mobile apps → $42B moved in hours. The psychological mechanism (fear → withdrawal → collapse) is the same as 1907, but the velocity has increased by orders of magnitude.
  • Polycrisis mentality / 多重危机心理: the psychological experience of the 2020s is one of overlapping, accelerating crises — financial, pandemic, climate, geopolitical. This creates a distinct mental state: a sense that the old order is crumbling, combined with uncertainty about what replaces it. Historically, such periods produce both dangerous extremism and creative institutional innovation.
  • AI anxiety / 人工智能焦虑: unlike previous technological disruptions, AI threatens cognitive work — the domain of educated professionals who thought they were immune to automation. The psychological impact of this realization is still unfolding, but it promises to reshape career expectations, educational choices, and political alignments.
