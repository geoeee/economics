Financial Derivatives / 金融衍生品

Financial instruments whose value derives from an underlying asset — the most powerful and most dangerous tools ever invented for managing risk.

📈 Economics / 经济学

Why Derivatives Exist / 衍生品为何存在

Derivatives solve a fundamental economic problem: future prices are unknown. A wheat farmer doesn't know what wheat will be worth at harvest. A baker doesn't know what flour will cost in 6 months. Both face price risk. Derivatives transfer this risk from those who don't want it (hedgers) to those willing to bear it (speculators) — in theory, increasing economic efficiency by enabling specialization without price uncertainty.

The Four Basic Types / 四类基本衍生品

TypeWhat It IsPurposeExample
Forward / Futures
远期/期货		 Obligation to buy/sell at a set price on a future date Lock in price today for future delivery. Eliminates uncertainty for both sides. Airline locks in jet fuel price; farmer locks in corn price
Option
期权		 Right (not obligation) to buy (call) or sell (put) at a strike price Insurance — pay premium for downside protection, keep upside Fund manager buys put on S&P 500 as portfolio insurance
Swap
互换		 Exchange one set of cash flows for another Transform exposure: fixed→floating rate, currency, credit risk Company swaps variable-rate loan for fixed-rate payments
CDS / 信用违约互换 Insurance against bond default Protect against or speculate on credit events (bankruptcy) Hedge Greek sovereign debt exposure

Hedging vs Speculation / 套保 vs 投机

The same instrument used by a farmer to protect income is used by a trader to bet on price movements. The line blurs quickly. Southwest Airlines famously hedged fuel costs and saved billions; other airlines speculated on oil direction and lost billions. Derivatives amplify both gains and losses.

⚡ Politics / 政治

"Weapons of Mass Destruction" / 大规模杀伤性武器

Warren Buffett's famous warning (2002 Berkshire letter). After the 2008 crisis, derivatives — particularly CDOs (Collateralized Debt Obligations) and CDS (Credit Default Swaps) — became targets of public anger and regulatory reform.

Regulatory Response / 监管回应

  • Dodd-Frank Act (2010): mandated central clearing for standardized swaps, exchange trading where possible, position limits
  • EMIR (EU): similar European regulation. Reporting requirements, central clearing, risk mitigation
  • The Volcker Rule: restricted proprietary trading by deposit-taking banks — separating utility banking from speculative derivatives trading
The 2008 crisis revealed that derivatives had concentrated, not dispersed, risk. AIG sold ~$500B in CDS protection without the capital to back it. When housing collapsed, the entire system nearly went with it. The taxpayer bailout ($182B to AIG) showed that in complex, interconnected markets, "private" risk becomes public liability.
📜 History / 历史
Ancient
Forward contracts in Mesopotamia
Clay tablets recorded agreements for future delivery of grain at set prices — the original derivatives.
17th C
Dojima Rice Exchange (Japan)
World's first organized futures market. Samurai stipends paid in rice; futures allowed price risk management.
1848
CBOT founded (Chicago)
Chicago Board of Trade standardized grain futures. Standardization made markets liquid. The model spread globally.
1972
Currency futures (CME)
After Bretton Woods collapsed, floating exchange rates created demand for FX hedging. Milton Friedman's advocacy was crucial.
1973
Black-Scholes-Merton formula
The mathematical breakthrough that made options markets explode. Turned uncertainty into a tradeable, priceable commodity.
1990s–2000s
Derivatives explosion
Notional value of outstanding derivatives grew from ~$100T to ~$700T+. CDOs, CDS, MBS — the alphabet soup of the 2008 crisis.
💰 Finance / 金融

Key Concepts / 关键概念

Notional vs Market Value Global derivatives notional is ~$600T+, but net market value is much smaller (~$20T). Notional is the reference amount — misleading if quoted alone.
Counterparty Risk The risk that the other side of your derivative contract defaults. Central clearing (CCPs) mitigates this by standing between both parties.
Margin / Collateral Initial margin posted upfront; variation margin settles daily gains/losses. Under-collateralization = systemic risk.
Greeks Delta (price sensitivity), Gamma (delta's rate of change), Theta (time decay), Vega (volatility sensitivity), Rho (rate sensitivity). The risk manager's toolkit.
📐 Mathematics / 数学

Black-Scholes-Merton PDE / BS方程

∂V/∂t + (1/2)σ²S²(∂²V/∂S²) + rS(∂V/∂S) − rV = 0

V = option price
S = underlying asset price
σ = volatility
r = risk-free rate

Solved as: C = SN(d₁) − Ke^(−rT)N(d₂)

d₁ = [ln(S/K) + (r + σ²/2)T] / (σ√T)
d₂ = d₁ − σ√T

Put-Call Parity / 买卖权平价

C + Ke^(−rT) = P + S

C = call price, P = put price, K = strike, S = spot
If this doesn't hold → arbitrage exists
"No free lunch" enforced by this identity

Delta Hedging / 德尔塔对冲

Delta (Δ) = ∂V/∂S  ≈ change in option price per $1 move in stock

Delta-neutral portfolio: Σ Δ_i = 0
Market-neutral: no exposure to small price moves
Requires continuous rebalancing → transaction costs

Value at Risk (VaR) / 在险价值

VaR_α = minimum loss in worst (1−α) of cases

Parametric: VaR_95% = μ − 1.645σ (normal assumption)
Historical: rank actual returns, take 5th percentile
Monte Carlo: simulate 10,000 scenarios, take 500th worst
🧠 Psychology / 心理
  • Model risk / 模型风险: the psychological trap of confusing the model with reality. Black-Scholes assumes continuous trading, no transaction costs, constant volatility — none of which are true. Professionals know this; the danger is when they forget.
  • Overconfidence in mathematical precision: derivatives are priced to six decimal places based on assumptions with 50% error bars. The precision is an illusion — but a psychologically compelling one.
  • Complexity as camouflage / 复杂即伪装: financial engineers can construct derivatives so complex that even the buyers don't fully understand them. This information asymmetry isn't a bug — for sellers, it's often the feature.
  • Tail risk neglect / 尾部风险忽视: people systematically underestimate the probability of extreme events. 2008 was a "25-sigma event" according to models — meaning it should never happen. The problem wasn't the event; it was the models.
