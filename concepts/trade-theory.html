Trade Theory / 贸易理论

Why nations trade, who gains and who loses, and why free trade is simultaneously the most proven and most contested idea in economics.

📈 Economics / 经济学

Comparative Advantage / 比较优势

David Ricardo's 1817 insight: even if one country is better at producing everything (absolute advantage), both countries gain from trade if each specializes in what it produces relatively more efficiently. This is comparative advantage — one of the most profound and non-obvious truths in economics.

Example: If a lawyer is also the world's fastest typist, should they do their own typing? No — the lawyer has comparative advantage in law, even though they have absolute advantage in both. Trade frees them to do the higher-value work.

Heckscher-Ohlin Model / H-O模型

Refinement of comparative advantage: countries export goods that intensively use their abundant factors of production. Capital-rich countries (US, Germany) export capital-intensive goods; labor-rich countries (Bangladesh, Vietnam) export labor-intensive goods. This model predicts that trade benefits a country's abundant factor and hurts its scarce factor — explaining why labor in rich countries often opposes free trade.

New Trade Theory / 新贸易理论

Paul Krugman (Nobel 2008) explained why similar countries trade with each other (US ↔ Germany, not just US ↔ Bangladesh). Answer: economies of scale + consumer love of variety. Even countries with identical factor endowments gain from specializing in different brands/products, reducing average costs through scale.

⚡ Politics / 政治

Free Trade vs Protectionism / 自由贸易 vs 保护主义

The intellectual case for free trade is nearly unanimous among economists. The political case is perpetually contested. Why? Because trade's benefits are diffuse (all consumers pay slightly less) while its costs are concentrated (factory workers lose jobs). Concentrated losers organize politically; diffuse winners don't.

Trade Wars / 贸易战

The US-China trade war (2018–) marked a dramatic reversal of decades of trade liberalization. Tariffs function as a tax on domestic consumers: studies found US tariffs were almost entirely passed through to US importers and consumers — not absorbed by Chinese exporters as claimed.

The Smoot-Hawley Tariff (1930) raised US tariffs to historic highs. Trading partners retaliated. Global trade collapsed by ~65%. The lesson was learned — until 2018.

💰 Finance / 金融

Trade imbalances correspond to capital flows: a trade deficit (importing more than exporting) means a country is selling assets to foreigners or borrowing from them. The US trade deficit = the world's demand for US dollar assets. This is the Triffin dilemma: the global reserve currency issuer must run persistent trade deficits to supply the world with its currency.

Sovereign wealth funds and foreign exchange reserves are the financial manifestation of trade surpluses. China's ~$3T reserves, Saudi Arabia's PIF, Norway's Oil Fund — all are accumulated trade surpluses reinvested globally.

📐 Mathematics / 数学

Comparative Advantage / 比较优势

$$\begin{aligned} \text{Country A: can produce } &10W \text{ or } 5C \text{ per hour} \\ \text{Country B: can produce } &4W \text{ or } 4C \text{ per hour} \\[4pt] \text{Opportunity cost:} \\ A\!: \quad 1W &= 0.5C, \quad 1C = 2W \\ B\!: \quad 1W &= 1C, \quad\;\; 1C = 1W \\[4pt] A &\text{ has lower opp. cost in } W \rightarrow \text{ specialize in } W \\ B &\text{ has lower opp. cost in } C \rightarrow \text{ specialize in } C \\ &\text{Both gain from trade at rate between } 0.5C \text{ and } 1C \text{ per } W \end{aligned}$$

Terms of Trade / 贸易条件

$$\begin{aligned} \text{ToT} &= \frac{\text{Export Price Index}}{\text{Import Price Index}} \times 100 \\[4pt] \text{ToT} > 100 &\rightarrow \text{ export prices rose relative to imports (improvement)} \\ \text{ToT} < 100 &\rightarrow \text{ export prices fell relative to imports (deterioration)} \end{aligned}$$

Gravity Model / 引力模型

$$\begin{aligned} \text{Trade}_{ij} &\approx G \times \frac{\text{GDP}_i \times \text{GDP}_j}{\text{Distance}_{ij}} \\[4pt] &\text{Trade between two countries is proportional to their economic sizes} \\ &\text{and inversely proportional to distance (like Newton's gravity).} \end{aligned}$$
🧠 Psychology / 心理
  • Foreign bias / 外国偏见: consumers systematically undervalue foreign goods relative to domestic equivalents, even when quality is identical. "Buy American" sentiment creates a psychological tariff.
  • Mercantilist intuition / 重商主义直觉: the layperson's instinct that exports = good and imports = bad. Economists have fought this fallacy since Adam Smith — and largely lost the public. The intuitive but wrong mental model: trade is a zero-sum competition, not mutually beneficial exchange.
  • Loss aversion in trade / 贸易中的损失厌恶: the pain of losing a manufacturing job to trade (~$100K lifetime earnings loss) is psychologically far more salient than the diffuse benefit of cheaper goods (~$10K/year per household). This 10:1 concentration of pain vs. diffusion of gain makes trade politically fragile.
