Game Theory / 博弈论

The mathematical study of strategic interaction — how rational actors make decisions when outcomes depend on the choices of others.

📈 Economics / 经济学

Why Game Theory Matters for Economics

Classical economics treated firms as isolated optimizers facing a fixed market price. Game theory (developed by von Neumann, Morgenstern, and Nash) revealed that in concentrated markets — oligopolies — firms are locked in strategic interdependence. Your best move depends on what you expect your competitor to do, which depends on what they expect you to do, ad infinitum.

Prisoner's Dilemma / 囚徒困境

The most famous game in economics: two prisoners, interrogated separately. If both stay silent, both get 1 year. If one confesses and the other doesn't, the confessor goes free and the other gets 10 years. If both confess, both get 5 years. The dominant strategy for each individual is to confess — yet mutual confession yields a worse outcome for both than mutual silence.

Economic parallel: Price wars. If both firms keep prices high, both earn healthy profits. But each has incentive to undercut the other. If both undercut, both earn less. If collusion were legal, they'd coordinate on high prices. Antitrust law exists precisely to prevent this.

Other Key Games / 其他关键博弈

Chicken / 胆小鬼博弈 Two cars drive at each other; first to swerve "loses." Neither wants mutual collision (worst outcome). Modeling brinkmanship in nuclear deterrence, labor strikes.
Coordination Game Both players prefer to coordinate on the same choice (driving side, tech standards). Multiple Nash equilibria. The problem isn't conflict — it's alignment.
Battle of the Sexes Both prefer to be together, but each prefers a different activity. Models compromise situations where communication helps.
Ultimatum Game Proposer splits $100; responder accepts or both get nothing. Rational solution: offer $0.01. Real-world: offers below ~$30 are rejected — people punish unfairness at their own cost.
⚡ Politics / 政治

Nuclear Deterrence / 核威慑

The Cold War was a giant game of Chicken expressed as Mutually Assured Destruction (MAD). The Nash equilibrium: both sides maintain second-strike capability, neither launches first. Thomas Schelling won the Nobel Prize for applying game theory to conflict and deterrence — showing that the ability to commit credibly to retaliation changes the game.

Collective Action Problem / 集体行动困境

Climate change is the largest game theory problem humanity faces: every nation benefits from others cutting emissions but prefers not to bear the cost itself (free-riding). Without enforcement mechanisms, the dominant strategy is to defect — precisely the tragedy of the global commons.

📜 History / 历史
1838
Cournot Duopoly Model
Antoine Cournot modeled two firms competing on quantity — precursor to Nash equilibrium. Each firm's output is its best response to the other's.
1944
Theory of Games and Economic Behavior
von Neumann & Morgenstern publish the foundational text. Introduces expected utility theory and zero-sum game analysis.
1950
Nash Equilibrium
John Nash (age 21) proves every finite game has at least one equilibrium in mixed strategies. Transforms economics.
1994
Nobel Prize: Nash, Harsanyi, Selten
Recognition for game theory's transformation of economic analysis. Harsanyi (Bayesian games), Selten (subgame perfection).
2005
Nobel Prize: Aumann & Schelling
Conflict and cooperation through game-theoretic analysis. Schelling's work on credible commitment changed military strategy.
💰 Finance / 金融

Financial markets are giant multiplayer games. Keynesian beauty contest: investors don't pick what they think is best — they pick what they think others will think is best. This creates self-fulfilling bubbles and crashes.

Auction theory — a branch of mechanism design — designs the rules for how goods are sold when buyers have private information about value. From Treasury bond auctions to Google's ad auctions to 5G spectrum, auction design determines prices, efficiency, and revenue. William Vickrey (1996 Nobel) and Paul Milgrom & Robert Wilson (2020 Nobel) are the pioneers.

📐 Mathematics / 数学

Nash Equilibrium / 纳什均衡

$$\text{A strategy profile } (s_1^*, s_2^*, \ldots, s_n^*) \text{ is a Nash equilibrium if:} \\ u_i(s_i^*, s_{-i}^*) \geq u_i(s_i, s_{-i}^*) \quad \forall i, \forall s_i \\ \text{No player can improve by unilaterally changing strategy}$$

Prisoner's Dilemma Payoff Matrix

$$\begin{array}{c|cc} & \text{Cooperate} & \text{Defect} \\ \hline \text{Cooperate} & (-1,-1) & (-10,0) \\ \text{Defect} & (0,-10) & (-5,-5) \end{array} \\[4pt] \text{Defect dominates: } -5 > -10 \text{ and } 0 > -1 \\ \text{But } (-1,-1) \text{ Pareto-dominates } (-5,-5)$$

Mixed Strategy / 混合策略

$$\text{If no pure-strategy equilibrium exists, there is always} \\ \text{a mixed-strategy Nash equilibrium where players randomize} \\ \text{Payoff from strategy A = probability-weighted average of outcomes} \\ \text{Set equal to solve for equilibrium probabilities}$$

Expected Utility / 期望效用

$$\begin{aligned} EU &= \sum_i p_i \cdot u(x_i) \\[4pt] p_i &= \text{probability of outcome } i \\ u(x_i) &= \text{utility from outcome } i \\[4pt] \text{Risk aversion: } & u'' < 0 \text{ (diminishing marginal utility)} \end{aligned}$$
🧠 Psychology / 心理
  • Trust and reputation / 信任与声誉: in repeated games, cooperation emerges even among self-interested players through tit-for-tat strategies (Axelrod, 1984). "I'll cooperate if you did last round." This explains how trust evolves without a central authority — the foundation of all trade and markets.
  • Bounded rationality / 有限理性: real humans don't compute Nash equilibria. Herbert Simon: we satisfice (seek satisfactory, not optimal, outcomes) due to cognitive limits. This is why simple heuristics often beat complex game-theoretic reasoning in real-world settings.
  • Fairness preference / 公平偏好: the Ultimatum Game reveals that humans are not purely self-interested. We sacrifice our own gains to punish unfairness — an evolved capacity for norm enforcement that undergirds all cooperation.
