Fiscal Policy / 财政政策

Government spending, taxation, and borrowing — the tools by which the state manages aggregate demand and redistributes resources.

📈 Economics / 经济学

The Fiscal Toolbox / 财政工具箱

Government spending Infrastructure, defense, education, social programs — directly adds to GDP
Taxation Income tax, corporate tax, VAT, tariffs — reduces disposable income but funds services
Transfers Welfare, unemployment benefits, pensions — redistributes without direct production
Borrowing Issuing government bonds to fund deficits; future taxes pay the interest

Expansionary vs Contractionary / 扩张 vs 紧缩

Expansionary fiscal policy (increased spending or tax cuts) boosts aggregate demand during recessions. Contractionary fiscal policy (spending cuts or tax hikes) cools an overheating economy and reduces deficits during booms.

Political reality: expansion is easy and popular; contraction is politically painful. This creates a structural bias toward deficits.

Automatic Stabilizers / 自动稳定器

Some fiscal mechanisms engage without new legislation: during recessions, tax revenues fall (lower incomes) and welfare payments rise (more unemployment claims) — automatically injecting demand. During booms, revenues rise and welfare costs fall — automatically cooling growth. This is counter-cyclical fiscal policy on autopilot.

⚡ Politics / 政治

The Politics of Deficits / 赤字的政治

Fiscal policy is where economics most directly becomes politics. Key debates:

  • Size of government: what share of GDP should the state control? Nordic countries ~50%; Singapore ~15%.
  • Progressive vs flat tax: should higher earners pay higher rates? Raises questions of fairness, incentives, and revenue optimization.
  • Deficit spending: Keynesians argue deficits during recessions are essential; austerity advocates argue they burden future generations and crowd out private investment.
  • Debt ceiling: unique US political mechanism that periodically threatens sovereign default — a self-imposed crisis.

MMT Perspective / 现代货币理论视角

Modern Monetary Theory argues that monetarily sovereign nations can't go bankrupt — they can always create money to pay debts. The real constraint is inflation, not solvency. This reframes fiscal policy from "can we afford it?" to "will it cause inflation?"

💰 Finance / 金融

Government bonds are the bedrock of global finance. US Treasuries are the "risk-free rate" against which all other assets are priced. Fiscal credibility determines borrowing costs: the US borrows at ~4-5%; Argentina at ~20%+.

The debt-to-GDP ratio is the key solvency metric. A country can sustainably run deficits if its GDP grows faster than its interest payments accumulate. This is the r < g condition (interest rate < growth rate) — when it holds, debt shrinks relative to the economy even without surpluses.

📐 Mathematics / 数学

Fiscal Multiplier / 财政乘数

$$\begin{aligned} \text{Multiplier} &= \frac{1}{1 - \text{MPC}} \\[4pt] \text{MPC} &= \text{Marginal Propensity to Consume} \\ \text{If MPC} &= 0.8 \;\rightarrow\; \text{Multiplier} = 5 \\ \$1 \text{ of government spending} &\rightarrow \$5 \text{ of GDP} \end{aligned}$$

Debt Dynamics / 债务动态

$$\begin{aligned} \Delta d &= d(r - g) - pb \\[4pt] d &= \text{debt/GDP ratio} \\ r &= \text{interest rate on debt} \\ g &= \text{GDP growth rate} \\ pb &= \text{primary balance/GDP (surplus positive)} \\[4pt] &\text{If } r < g\text{, debt/GDP can decline even with primary deficits} \end{aligned}$$

Laffer Curve / 拉弗曲线

$$\begin{aligned} \text{Tax Revenue} &= \text{tax\_rate} \times \text{tax\_base} \\[4pt] \text{At } 0\% \text{ rate} &\rightarrow \$0 \text{ revenue} \\ \text{At } 100\% \text{ rate} &\rightarrow \$0 \text{ revenue (no incentive to work)} \\ &\text{Maximum revenue at some intermediate rate} \end{aligned}$$
🧠 Psychology / 心理
  • Fiscal illusion / 财政幻觉: voters perceive the benefits of government spending but underestimate its costs (taxes, inflation, future debt burden). Politicians exploit this asymmetry.
  • Ricardian equivalence / 李嘉图等价: a theoretical argument that tax cuts today don't stimulate demand because rational consumers save the windfall, anticipating future tax hikes to pay the debt. Behavioral evidence: people are not that rational — tax cuts do stimulate spending.
  • Fairness / 公平感: tax compliance depends heavily on perceived fairness. If people believe others cheat or the rich pay less, compliance collapses. Trust in institutions is the invisible foundation of tax systems.
  • Loss framing / 损失框架: "tax relief" frames government revenue as a loss. This linguistic framing has been remarkably effective in shifting political attitudes toward lower taxes.
