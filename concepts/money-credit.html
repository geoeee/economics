Money & Credit / 货币与信用

What is money? How is it created? And why does credit — not coin — drive the modern economic engine?

📈 Economics / 经济学

The Three Functions of Money / 货币三大职能

Medium of exchange / 交换媒介 Solves the double coincidence of wants problem in barter
Unit of account / 计价单位 Common measure for pricing goods, recording debts, accounting
Store of value / 价值储藏 Preserves purchasing power across time (undermined by inflation)

What Counts as Money? / 什么是货币？

MeasureIncludes
M0 (Monetary Base)Physical currency + bank reserves at central bank
M1M0 + demand deposits (checking accounts)
M2M1 + savings deposits, money market funds, small time deposits
M3M2 + large time deposits, institutional funds (no longer published by Fed)

Credit Creation / 信用创造

Modern money is predominantly bank-created credit. When a bank issues a loan, it creates a deposit — new money enters the economy. This is the endogenous money view: commercial banks, not the central bank, create most money through lending.

Key insight: Banks don't lend out existing deposits. They create deposits by lending. Every loan creates a matching deposit — and new money.

Fractional Reserve Banking / 部分准备金制度

Banks hold only a fraction of deposits as reserves. The money multiplier (1/reserve ratio) determines maximum money creation from a given base. In practice, many central banks no longer enforce reserve requirements — instead using interest rates on reserves to influence lending.

⚡ Politics / 政治

Central Bank Independence / 央行独立性

Who controls money creation? The tension between elected governments (who want cheap money before elections) and independent central banks (who prioritize price stability) is a defining political-economic conflict. Most developed nations have granted their central banks operational independence — setting interest rates without political interference — but goal dependence (parliament sets the inflation target).

Currency Sovereignty / 货币主权

Countries that issue their own currency and borrow in it (US, Japan, UK) cannot technically "run out of money" — they can always create more. Countries that borrow in foreign currency (Argentina, many developing nations) face genuine default risk. This asymmetry is at the heart of Modern Monetary Theory (MMT) debates.

💰 Finance / 金融

Credit markets are the circulatory system of modern capitalism. Yield curves (the spread between short and long-term interest rates) signal market expectations about future growth and inflation. An inverted yield curve (short-term rates higher than long-term) has preceded every US recession since 1950.

Shadow banking — lending by non-bank institutions (money market funds, hedge funds, private credit) — now exceeds traditional bank lending in many economies. This creates credit outside regulatory oversight, a key vulnerability in the 2008 crisis.

📐 Mathematics / 数学

Money Multiplier / 货币乘数

Money Supply = Monetary Base × Money Multiplier
M = B × m

where m = 1 / r  (r = reserve ratio, simplified)

With r = 10%: m = 10 → $1 in base creates $10 in broad money

Quantity Theory of Money / 货币数量论

MV = PY

M = Money supply
V = Velocity of money (how often $1 is spent)
P = Price level
Y = Real output

If V and Y are constant: ΔM → ΔP (inflation)

Present Value / 现值

PV = FV / (1 + r)^n

r = interest rate (discount rate)
n = number of periods
FV = future value

The core of all lending: money today is worth more than money tomorrow.
🧠 Psychology / 心理
  • Money illusion / 货币幻觉: people think in nominal terms (dollar amounts) rather than real terms (purchasing power). A 2% raise during 3% inflation feels like a gain but is a real loss.
  • Hyperbolic discounting / 双曲线贴现: humans massively overvalue immediate rewards. This explains the appeal of loans — "buy now, pay later" exploits our psychological bias toward the present.
  • Mental accounting / 心理账户: we treat money differently based on its source and intended use. "Found money" (tax refunds, bonuses) is spent more freely than earned income.
  • Trust: all money is fundamentally a social convention. When trust collapses (hyperinflation, bank runs), the value of money evaporates — no matter its physical properties.
