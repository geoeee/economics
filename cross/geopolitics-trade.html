Geopolitics & Trade / 地缘政治与贸易

How geography, military power, and political rivalries shape the global economic order — and why trade is never just about economics.

📈 Economics / 经济学

The Economic Tools of Geopolitics / 地缘政治的经济工具

Sanctions / 制裁 Economic warfare without bullets. Freeze assets, ban trade, block SWIFT. Effectiveness debated — Cuba (60+ years, limited success), Iran (nuclear deal), Russia (2022-, partial).
Tariffs / 关税 Tax on imports for political ends: protect domestic industry, punish rivals, raise revenue. US-China trade war (2018-) raised average US tariffs on Chinese goods from ~3% to ~19%.
Export Controls / 出口管制 Blocking sale of critical technology (semiconductors, AI chips). US restrictions on Huawei, ASML lithography machines, advanced NVIDIA GPUs to China. The weaponization of supply chains.
Investment Screening CFIUS (US) and similar bodies review foreign acquisitions for national security risk. Chinese investment in Western tech infrastructure increasingly blocked.

Supply Chain Weaponization / 供应链武器化

Modern economies depend on global supply chains — semiconductors from Taiwan, rare earths from China, energy from the Middle East and Russia. Chokepoints (Strait of Malacca, Suez Canal, Strait of Hormuz) are geographic vulnerabilities. The post-2022 push for reshoring, friend-shoring, and supply chain redundancy is fundamentally a geopolitical response to economic vulnerability.

⚡ Politics / 政治

US-China Strategic Competition / 中美战略竞争

The defining geopolitical-economic conflict of the 21st century. Key economic fronts:

  • Technology race: AI, semiconductors, quantum computing, biotech — whoever leads sets global standards
  • Belt and Road Initiative (BRI): China's infrastructure-led economic diplomacy across 140+ countries; ~$1T in projects
  • Reserve currency competition: dollar dominance vs yuan internationalization (CIPS, digital yuan, commodity contracts in RMB)
  • Decoupling vs de-risking: the US speaks of "de-risking" not decoupling; China sees it as containment regardless of label

The Dollar Weapon / 美元武器

US dollar dominance grants extraterritorial legal reach — any transaction touching the US financial system (clearing, correspondent banking) falls under US jurisdiction. This allows sanctions with global reach. The response: de-dollarization — bilateral currency swaps, gold reserve increases (central banks bought record gold in 2022–23), and alternative payment systems (Russia's SPFS, China's CIPS).

💰 Finance / 金融

Energy and the Petrodollar / 能源与石油美元

The 1974 US-Saudi agreement — oil priced exclusively in dollars, recycled through US assets — created the petrodollar system. It created structural demand for dollars and Treasuries independent of US economic fundamentals. As the energy transition accelerates and Saudi Arabia diversifies (pricing some oil in RMB), this pillar of dollar dominance erodes.

Sovereign Wealth Funds as Geopolitical Tools

Norway's $1.7T fund, China's $3T reserves, Gulf SWFs — these are financial instruments with geopolitical weight. Investment decisions (what to buy, what to divest) signal alignment. The rise of "values-based investing" at SWFs blends finance with foreign policy.

Optimal Tariff / 最优关税

Optimal tariff = 1 / ε*

ε* = foreign export supply elasticity

If foreign supply is inelastic (can't easily redirect exports),
an importing country can extract terms-of-trade gains via tariffs.
But this invites retaliation → both lose (trade war).

🧠 Psychology / 心理
  • In-group / out-group bias: "Buy American" / "Buy Chinese" campaigns leverage tribalism that evolved when trade happened within kin groups. Opposition to trade is partly a psychological mismatch: our brains evolved for within-tribe exchange, not global supply chains.
  • Zero-sum bias / 零和偏差: the instinct that one nation's gain is another's loss. Trade is positive-sum, but this feels counterintuitive. Politicians exploit the zero-sum intuition for protectionist support.
  • Narrative of decline / 衰落叙事: a powerful psychological driver of trade conflict. "They're taking our jobs" resonates whether or not it's economically accurate. Economic anxiety is more politically potent than economic data.
